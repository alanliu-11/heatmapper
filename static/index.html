Term definitions
Call Wall The strike above spot with the largest positive net GEX. Dealers are most heavily long gamma here, so they fade rallies by selling — acts as a ceiling. Price often struggles to sustain above it.
Put Wall The strike below spot with the largest positive net GEX. Dealers long gamma here buy dips — acts as a floor. The strongest downside support in the GEX framework.
2nd Call / Put Wall The second-largest positive GEX strike above or below spot. If the primary wall breaks, this is the next expected zone of stabilisation.
Gamma Flip The strike closest to spot where net GEX changes sign (positive ↔ negative). Above it dealers are net long gamma (stabilising). Below it they are net short gamma (destabilising). Crossing the flip shifts the market from mean-reverting to trending.
Acceleration Zone A strike between spot and a wall where net GEX is negative. Dealers short gamma here must chase the move to stay hedged — selling into a falling price, buying into a rising one. Instead of resisting a move toward the wall, they amplify it.
VEX Magnet The strike within ~3% of spot with the largest positive net VEX. As IV compresses, dealers lose delta and sell, pulling price toward this strike. A gravitational centre for post-event drift.
VEX Repellent The strike within ~3% of spot with the largest negative net VEX. As IV falls dealers buy to re-hedge, pushing price away. Tends to repel price during vol compression.
VEX Drift Target The largest positive VEX strike across the whole board. After a vol event (earnings, Fed, CPI), IV crush drives the biggest vanna unwind here — dealers sell delta at scale, gravitating price toward this level over hours to days.
Net GEX Dealer net gamma exposure at a strike in dollars, summed across all expirations: (call gamma − put gamma) × OI × 100 × spot² × 0.01. Positive = dealers stabilise price. Negative = dealers amplify moves.
Net VEX Dealer net vanna exposure at a strike. Vanna measures how delta changes as IV moves. Positive VEX means dealers gain delta when IV rises. Drives directional pressure during vol expansions and crushes.
GEX − VEX Diff The difference between net GEX and net VEX at each strike, tracked on the chart. Where they diverge, gamma and vanna forces pull in different directions — the level is contested. Where they align, both flows reinforce the same behaviour.
Stable regime Spot is above the gamma flip. Dealers are net long gamma in aggregate — they dampen volatility by fading moves. The market tends to mean-revert within the walls.
Volatile regime Spot is below the gamma flip. Dealers are net short gamma — they amplify moves by chasing them. Directional breaks are more likely to extend and realised vol tends to be higher.
GEX/VEX Aligned vs Conflict Whether gamma and vanna forces near spot point the same direction. Aligned = both flows reinforce the same hedging bias. Conflict = one stabilises while the other drives directional flow — net effect is ambiguous.
